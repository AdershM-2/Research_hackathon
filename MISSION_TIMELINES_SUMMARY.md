## Phase-by-Phase Analysis

### PHASE 1: Hohmann Transfer (400 km → 600 km)
**Duration: 47.23 minutes (constant across all configs)**

The Hohmann transfer time is determined by orbital mechanics and is independent of propulsion acceleration. However, higher acceleration reduces burn times:

| Configuration | Burn 1 | Coast | Burn 2 | Total |
|---------------|--------|-------|--------|-------|
| 10kg, 1C | 8.7 min | 30.0 min | 8.6 min | 47.2 min |
| 10kg, 5C | 1.7 min | 43.8 min | 1.7 min | 47.2 min |
| 25kg, 5C | 4.3 min | 38.6 min | 4.3 min | 47.2 min |

**Key Insight:** Higher charge levels dramatically reduce burn times, increasing coast time and reducing propellant-equivalent expenditure.

## DEORBIT PHASE ANALYSIS

### PHASE 6a: Deorbit Burn (600 km → 250 km perigee)
**Duration: 18-115 minutes (depends on configuration)**

After capturing the debris, the combined system (chaser + debris) performs a retrograde burn to lower the perigee to 250 km.

**Combined System Performance:**

| Configuration | Combined Mass | Combined Accel | Required Δv | Burn Time |
|---------------|---------------|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| 10kg + 50kg debris | 60 kg | 0.089 m/s² | 98.0 m/s | 18.3 min |
| 25kg + 50kg debris | 75 kg | 0.071 m/s² | 98.0 m/s | 22.9 min |

**Fastest:** 10kg, 5C → 18.3 minutes
**Slowest:** 25kg, 1C → 114.3 minutes

**Key Insight:** Lighter chasers with higher charge maintain better acceleration even after capturing heavy debris.

---

### PHASE 6b: Atmospheric Decay (250 km → reentry)
**Duration: ~2 days (atmospheric drag-dependent)**

Once the perigee is lowered to 250 km, atmospheric drag naturally decays the orbit over approximately 2 days.

**Factors Affecting Decay Time:**
- Solar activity (higher = faster decay)
- Debris area-to-mass ratio
- Atmospheric density variations
- Perigee altitude (250 km chosen for ~2 day decay)

**Alternative Options:**
- Lower to 200 km perigee → ~12 hours decay (more aggressive)
- Lower to 300 km perigee → ~7 days decay (more conservative)

**Current Choice:** 250 km provides good balance between:
- Rapid deorbit (2 days acceptable)
- Reasonable propellant expenditure
- Margin for errors

## Operational Considerations

### Advantages

1. **Speed:** Mission completed in ~2 days vs weeks
2. **Efficiency:** 99.7% energy margin remaining
3. **Flexibility:** Can adjust thrust levels in real-time
4. **Safety:** Gentle approaches with precise control
5. **Repeatability:** Can perform multiple missions with same chaser

### Challenges

1. **Detumbling:** Torque capability needs optimization
2. **Charge maintenance:** Space plasma may neutralize charges
3. **Orbital position:** Magnetic field varies with location
4. **Debris charging:** Need reliable method to charge debris

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **Torque:** Increase shell separation to 10-20m
2. **Charge maintenance:** Active charging system, monitoring
3. **Orbital planning:** Pre-compute optimal burn locations
4. **Debris charging:** Electron beam, ion beam, or contact methods
